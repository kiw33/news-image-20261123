## The six panels

1. **NATO meets on Poland missiles** — Polish authorities confirmed on
   Sunday 22 Nov that the three flying objects that fell in south-eastern
   Poland on 20 Nov were all Russian Kh-101 cruise missiles (Lublin
   prosecutor's office; fragments examined, produced Q4 2026 in Dubna).
   PM Donald Tusk called it an "intentional provocation". NATO's North
   Atlantic Council holds an extraordinary meeting in Brussels on Monday
   23 Nov (EU Today, Europe Says, GeoXAxis, 23 Nov).
2. **Russian warship capsizes in Libya** — Suspected Ukrainian sea drones
   hit the Russian corvette Merkury at the Benghazi naval base on Sunday
   22 Nov. Libyan officials say the ship was severely damaged and later
   capsized; 10 people injured. Neither Kyiv nor Moscow has confirmed
   responsibility (AP / Reuters / BBC Verify, via 23 Nov round-up).
3. **Tankers hit in the Strait of Hormuz** — At least three oil tankers
   were struck in and near the Strait of Hormuz on Friday and Saturday,
   and Iran's navy said two more tankers caught fire on Sunday after
   hitting naval mines. UK maritime authorities (UKMTO) reported
   projectile strikes. Attacks on shipping in the area are at their
   highest level since the war began (AP / CNN-UKMTO analysis / IRNA,
   22 Nov, reported 23 Nov).
4. **Drone hits flats near Kyiv** — A Russian drone hit a five-storey
   apartment block in Dymer, north of Kyiv, early on Sunday 22 Nov,
   killing 2 people and leaving 11 missing, including two children.
   Rescuers were still searching the rubble (Al Jazeera / AP, 22 Nov).
5. **COP31 collapses in Antalya** — The COP31 climate conference
   collapsed on Sunday 22 Nov as countries failed to agree an overarching
   deal after more than two weeks; the breaking point was fossil fuels —
   the final text did not mention them. The EU, UK, Panama, Marshall
   Islands and Norway were among those that rejected it. Longest COP in
   history and the first major breakdown since COP15 in 2009 (CNN,
   Le Monde, WSJ, The Times, 22–23 Nov).
6. **BepiColombo reaches Mercury** — After an eight-year journey, the
   European-Japanese BepiColombo spacecraft entered orbit around Mercury
   on 21 November — only the second mission ever to do so (23 Nov
   round-up).
